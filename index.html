  • Choices with Just-Noticeable Differences of Status Quo - Is There a (Noticeable) Difference? (2018)

    I propose a choice theory that explains the well-documented phenomenon of status quo bias (SQB) with the concept of just-noticeable differences (JNDs). I show that choice behaviour consistent with SQB can also be represented by JND utility, and propose a generalised representation of SQB with the classic JND utility representation.

Work in Progress

  • Consumer Theory for the Badly Behaved

    (joint with Joshua Lanier)

    We introduce a new method suitable for analyzing consumers who do not satisfy traditional revealed preference axioms. We show that to every dataset of consumer behavior there exists a utility function which not only explains the data but is also better-behaved (in some appropriately defined sense) than every other utility function which also explains the data. We show how these “best-behaved” utility functions can be used to make statements about consumer well-being and to make demand predictions. We also provide a method to quantify the degree to which the consumer fails to act as a well-behaved utility maximizer. The advantage of our approach is (i) it can be used even when the consumer does not act according to revealed preference axioms, (ii) it agrees with the traditional approach to consumer theory when the consumer does satisfy these axioms, and (iii) all of our methods are easy to implement in practice.

  • Morning or Evening person? Circadian Mismatch and Intraday Timing of Experiments (2022)

    (joint with Dongwoo Lee)

    Subject heterogeneity is among the most critical issues to address when conducting experiments. We consider chronotype - the natural inclination of our bodies to sleep at certain times - an essential source of heterogeneity when considering the intraday timing of experiments. Our experimental design involves testing the effects of chronotype mismatching on effort-based, cognitive-based, and attention-based tasks, as well as the mostly commonly used games and tasks in experiments.
